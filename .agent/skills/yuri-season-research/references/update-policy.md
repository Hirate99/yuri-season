# Routine editorial work

Routine combines complete current-season due-source coverage with autonomous discovery and publication, allowing major non-current-season exceptions. The in-scope watchlist is a coverage floor, not the boundary of reporting. Choose the order of coverage, discovery, and publication while remaining responsible for all in-scope due coverage and qualified candidates.

## Scope and priority

- At the start, resolve the website's current season from fresh season/catalog data. The website selects by the date in `Asia/Tokyo` and season `startsOn`, retaining the latest available season until a newer one begins; use its computed `isCurrent` and the corresponding work membership. Do not hard-code season names, switch a season to current for research, or infer membership from an account's recent activity. Record the selected season and included works in the existing handoff. If season data is unavailable or inconsistent, retain the unresolved selection and continue independently verifiable stories without claiming full seasonal coverage.
- The default mandatory source set is the enabled registered official sites/feeds and verified official/project, cast, original-author/creator, and production-staff accounts associated with current-season catalog works. An account's association does not make all its posts relevant. Current-season catalog membership, rather than an individual work's finished or not-yet-aired status, defines the baseline; directly related cross-season continuation news remains eligible.
- Prioritize broadcast/distribution and episode changes, postponements, PV/KV and new official visuals, production announcements, work-related interviews, music, and attendance/viewing events. Original manga updates, creator art/process, fanwork, merchandise, and project-member activity supplement coverage when directly related to the current-season anime and independently worthwhile. Shared cast, a generic tag, or franchise affiliation alone does not establish that relationship.
- Permit major news for non-current-season works within 百合季's editorial remit: verified new adaptations, sequels or films; firm premiere/release dates or material postponements/cancellations; major official PV/KV or production announcements; and significant work-related events with concrete reader value. Handle discovered originals and focused follow-ups, including user-designated leads, without scanning every old/future work or account. Ordinary reminders, routine merchandise, standalone fanwork, and unrelated personal activity do not qualify for this exception. Record the concrete reason for each exception; no publication quota or percentage is required.
- Necessary corrections to existing public content remain actionable regardless of season, especially incorrect times, access, cancellation status, attribution, or media. Explicit user requests and scoped discovery/audit/repair assignments override the default seasonal scope. Encountered notable events still follow `event-calendar-policy.md`; routine does not expand into an annual calendar audit.
- Preserve non-current-season unfinished evidence and source cursors. Bring a historical item into this run only when it concerns a current-season work, meets the major-news exception, requires a public correction, or is explicitly requested. Keep other backlog deferred with its existing evidence and next action; do not mark it ignored, covered, or complete merely because the season changed.

## Fixed coverage

- Process every in-scope due enabled registered official site, NEWS page, and first-party feed/API; open changed originals rather than relying on diff text. For shared sources, judge relevance per original without expanding the mandatory work set.
- Inspect all in-scope due verified, enabled X accounts across four lanes: official/project, cast, original authors/creators, and production staff. All four lanes remain mandatory; order them by urgency and reader value rather than allocating equal time or publication counts. Judge posts individually for their relationship to included works; low yield does not remove in-scope accounts from coverage.
- Use the committed cursor. Record each inspected original's stable ID and disposition, including ignored/rejected posts. Only reaching the previous cursor permits completion and advancement; an initial scan covers the previous 30 days. Save `resumeCursor` for partial scans. External articles and search snippets are not timeline evidence.
- When one X account appears under several works, inspect its timeline once through the oldest required in-scope boundary and reuse the captured evidence. Judge and record each included work's relevance separately; do not reopen the same account for every source row or advance an uninspected out-of-scope work's cursor.
- Recheck completely inspected in-scope X accounts after 3 hours. The recorder computes the interval; do not choose a next-check date or schedule reason. A recent check avoids redundant scanning but does not prevent following a new original, an event lead, or unfinished in-scope publication. Partial and blocked coverage remains unresolved; platform Retry-After still takes precedence. Visible out-of-scope accounts do not create mandatory checks.

## Autonomous discovery

Choose promising questions about current-season anime from recent developments and information gaps. Search for interviews, production discussions, creator output, and substantive event reporting. Follow originals, quoted posts, galleries, and necessary pagination while they add relevant evidence. Unregistered sites and unfamiliar accounts may supply verified story evidence without being enrolled in monitoring. Follow major non-current-season leads selectively under the scope rules rather than initiating a broad old/future-season sweep.

Discovery can happen alongside fixed coverage, without separate permission or a `discovery` campaign. Avoid repeating recent completed or zero-result searches. Stop searching when a lead becomes repetitive, irrelevant, or unlikely to yield evidence; prioritize ready publication and overdue coverage over speculative searches. Honor user-specified search budgets. Otherwise bound optional search sessions without treating that limit as a deadline for mandatory coverage or qualified publication. Explain briefly when no worthwhile question exists; there is no search or publication quota.

Use signed-in X search with evidenced official tags, current-season work names, and confirmed aliases to find official, creator, and supplementary fanwork posts beyond the watchlist. Choose searches from recent episodes, events, visuals, and information gaps; do not exhaust every tag each run. Verify the original author and stable post ID, deduplicate before further work, and apply the relevant sections of `research-policy.md`. A tag does not establish official identity. Record query, check time, inspected results, findings, and next action in existing editorial evidence; search sessions need not exhaust X and never substitute for in-scope timeline coverage. Resolve every qualified in-scope candidate actually found; preserve other leads as deferred evidence.

Bulk account enrollment, season-wide catalog audits, Pixiv/Instagram discovery, broad cross-work fanwork/community sweeps, and birthday audits remain separate assignments. Opening a relevant original and checking its authorship for a story is not bulk account discovery.

## Editorial judgment

Publish concrete reader value within the selected scope and priorities above. Current-season anime news leads; directly related manga chapters, creator art/process notes, fanwork, and substantive merchandise news are supplementary. Short captions can accompany meaningful visual storytelling; inspect media and quoted originals. A worthwhile historical post does not enter routine solely because it remains in an old queue.

For each relevant original, decide independently whether readers need a Feed update and whether its facts require an event, schedule, music, or other resource update. Attendance/viewing times and appearances trigger the event-policy check, not automatic event creation. A repeated reminder or already-published story may still fill a missing calendar entry or correct its time, access, participants, or status. Check the actual existing resource before concluding it is covered. Record the resource ID and action, or a brief not-applicable/unresolved reason in existing item evidence; no separate ledger or new schema is needed.

Ignore unrelated private activity and giveaways. Skip pure reposts and repetitive reminders as new Feed cards after resolving any relevant structured facts. Merge announcements of the same content through existing corrections, preserving source identity and dates. An interview is the story, with its referring post retained as discovery evidence. Apply [publication-policy.md](publication-policy.md) for copy, media, and verification; editorial review is the agent's responsibility.

Decide from each original's actual text, images, and quoted source before generating submission JSON. Builders may serialize those decisions, but must not default every hit to `ignored` or infer Feed rejection from the presence of an event ID. A merge names the existing matching publication; resource coverage names the matching resource and supported facts. Shared reasons are appropriate only for originals individually verified to belong to the same repetitive cluster. Unresolved originals remain work to adjudicate, not generic reminders.

## Browser access

Use only the user's connected Chrome for website, X, source-original and public-readback browsing. Read the current browser-control documentation and check this run's connection, supported APIs/capabilities and same-project ownership. Treat old handoff blockers as dated evidence to revalidate, not instructions to wait. Do not use the in-app browser, another browser/account, direct HTTP/fetch or the source-scan CLI as a substitute for Chrome webpage checks, or switch surfaces to evade throttling. Media file transfer is a separate operation: after verifying the original in Chrome and obtaining its actual media URL, prefer a local script to download the asset. Scripted public R2 asset GETs for byte/MIME/hash verification are also allowed; Feed/detail/Calendar webpage inspection remains in Chrome. Follow the media transfer rules in [publication-policy.md](publication-policy.md#media-file-transfer).

For scheduled task `x` only, the user explicitly authorizes opening Chrome through the official plugin to recover a failed connection; do not ask again for that launch permission. On `Browser is not available: chrome`, Chrome missing from connection discovery, or a failed lightweight Chrome call, read the currently installed official Chrome plugin's troubleshooting documentation. From that plugin root, use its supported Node runtime to run `scripts/open-chrome-window.js --browser chrome` once, opening a blank window in the profile selected by the official script. An unavailable provider does not establish that the Chrome process is stopped. An available official read-only process check is diagnostic; preserve a failed check's error without inferring that Chrome is stopped or automatically blocking independent launch/work.

After that single launch attempt, allow at most two connection-check rounds. In each round wait about two seconds, refresh `cua.getState()`, and retry the same documented lightweight Chrome call, such as `cua.listTabs({browser:"chrome"})`. Stop retrying as soon as it succeeds and resume ordinary sequential coverage after the existing state refresh. Do not repeatedly open windows. If the official script/tool is unavailable or launch is rejected, preserve the exact limitation, do not claim a launch occurred, and do not switch commands or interfaces to bypass a refusal. This authorization does not include installing/enabling extensions, logging in, changing browser/system permissions, security, sound or autoplay settings, evading throttling, taking over another research session, or switching browsers/accounts.

If connection recovery still fails, retain the actual affected work as incomplete, continue independent authorized work, and deliver a complete user-visible Chinese failure report before archiving. Include launch/retry counts, actual calls/commands and times, returned errors, completed and uncompleted coverage, production changes and public verification, recovery conditions and next actions. A local report/handoff file or a promise to report is not delivery; never silently exit or claim complete.

Create one dedicated research tab yourself with the currently documented Chrome tab-creation API, such as `browser.tabs.new()` or `cua.createBrowserTab`. A missing old research tab is not a prerequisite blocker or proof of lost login. Do not take over unrelated user tabs. Only X source inspection requires verifying the signed-in identity and reading the original timeline in that task tab; public official sites and public readbacks do not depend on X login or an existing X tab.

The user has withdrawn this task's muting requirement. Continue website/source/X timeline checks even when autoplay is enabled or no reliable per-tab mute API is available. Do not require pre-navigation muting, mute-capability probes or fallback handling, disabling autoplay, or proof that a page will remain silent. Conflicting mute/autoplay blockers in old handoffs or reports no longer apply. Do not change browser or system volume, mute or autoplay settings; preserve all other tool restrictions and permission boundaries.

Distinguish unavailable capabilities, unattempted operations and actual failed calls in evidence. Without an attempted call and its returned error, do not claim tab creation, login or navigation failed. Continue unaffected work; uninspected pages remain pending, never a no-change result. After genuine recovery, refresh context, committed receipts/cursors, ownership and current dedupe/public state before resuming coverage.

Pace X throughout the run, including successful browsing. Keep all X navigation, search, original-post opens and timeline loading sequential; do not parallelize them across tabs, browsers or agents. Allow at least 10 seconds between network-triggering navigations/searches/post opens, and at least 5 seconds between scrolls that load more posts. Wait longer when loading is slow. Read and adjudicate the loaded material before requesting the next page; reuse already visible originals instead of reopening or refreshing them. These are conservative local operating intervals, not documented X limits or a guarantee against throttling.

Treat a rendered “Something went wrong” or equivalent generic loading failure as possible throttling even without HTTP 429 or Retry-After. Their absence does not establish that requests are safe to continue. Inspect the current URL, login state and screenshot without reloading to distinguish an obvious login/browser problem; otherwise pause X requests for at least 5 minutes. Do not immediately refresh, test another account, change queries or switch browsers. During the pause, process captured evidence, official sites, ready publications and resources.

After the pause, make one recovery attempt in the same research tab. If the same error recurs, stop X for at least 30 minutes and preserve the actual resume point. Further failed recovery attempts extend the pause; do not repeat a short retry loop. Record this as suspected throttling, not confirmed HTTP 429. On explicit 429 or a platform rate-limit notice, honor Retry-After; when no retry time is supplied, wait at least 30 minutes before one recovery attempt. Never rotate accounts, sessions or browsers to evade a limit.

Save the error, observation time, last real post/cursor, attempted recovery and earliest retry time in existing editorial evidence. A new run does not reset the pause. Once it expires, verify recovery once and resume slowly if successful. A successful page does not justify a burst of queued requests. Recheck a user-reported recovery without overriding an active server-enforced cooldown. Keep the ordinary 3-hour source recheck interval separate from access backoff, and continue unaffected editorial work.

Keep access status and editorial disposition separate: an inaccessible source is blocked/partial, never ignored or a zero-result check. Preserve each task's actual progress; do not assign all unvisited accounts the failure of one account or invent resume cursors. Record attempted URL, time, browser surface, observed error, recovery attempts, and the real last inspected ID/cursor. Report unvisited tasks as pending, and report uninspected creator output as unverified rather than “no new art”.

## CLI operations

Routine has three commands, used independently:

| Action | Operation |
| --- | --- |
| Read catalog works, current-season X sources, cursors, recent publications/searches and unresolved evidence; apply this run's scope | `bun run research -- context` |
| Check registered official sources and retain unresolved changes | `bun run research -- sources` |
| Save inspected timeline evidence and synchronize existing search memory | `bun run research -- record <observations.json>` |

The context is an editorial briefing, not a list of permitted searches. Choose original sources, work names, verified tags, interviews and quoted chains yourself; keep exploratory query/result notes in the existing editorial journal. `needsCheck` uses the fixed 3-hour interval, not stored legacy next-search dates. No campaign, lease or finish command is needed for routine. Neither an empty change list nor recent source checks establish editorial completion.

Apply the seasonal scope to tool output before choosing work. The current X source plan is season-filtered, but `context.works`, historical hits/held candidates, and the registered-source selection may include other seasons; their presence does not expand mandatory coverage. Resolve membership from fresh catalog data when an output lacks `seasonId`. Preserve out-of-scope pending changes without resolving them as ignored. Tool output also does not override the Chrome browsing requirements above; do not use the source-scan CLI as a substitute for required webpage inspection.

Use the documented importer/Admin APIs for publications and resources. Website changes remain in `.research-cache/pending-diff.json` with version-specific `changeId` values. After Feed/resource decisions and public readbacks, run `bun run research:commit <resolutions.json>` with `{ "resolutions": [{ "changeId": "<exact changeId>", "outcome": "published", "reason": "<item-specific decision>", "evidenceUrls": ["<public readback URL>"] }] }`. Outcomes are `published`, `covered`, or `ignored`; published/covered require evidence URLs. Leave blocked versions unresolved.

See [discovery-results.md](discovery-results.md#routine-observations) for the small observation format. `record` validates and archives evidence before writing production memory. On an allowed retry, pass the original input or the saved `pendingEvidence` file to the same command; it reuses original inspection timestamps. A record receipt proves memory synchronization, not publication.

Context lists every unresolved memory hit as a brief lead; it does not establish that the story is still unpublished. Reconcile against production before preparing content. Use `context --details` when full historical text, media evidence or legacy cursors are needed. Original memory IDs and URLs remain available in the brief output.

Existing `update-plan.json` evidence is preserved. If context reports `legacyAwaitingSync`, replay it with `bun scripts/discovery-campaign.ts record --profile=routine`; `legacyUnfinished` identifies historical unfinished sources, not active leases or new tasks. Read their saved boundaries with `context --details` when resuming. Do not create or replace routine campaigns. Explicit discovery/account audits retain `scripts/full-discovery.ts` and `scripts/discovery-campaign.ts`.

## Operation failures and resumption

- Apply the [sandbox write gate](../SKILL.md#sandbox-write-gate) before recovery commands. In a sandbox, suspend all editorial writes and credential-refreshing commands; deliver unsaved evidence and handoff text in chat. This takes precedence over the file-saving and publication steps below; do not run a write to test whether restrictions apply.
- Retain original URLs, source IDs, inspection timestamps, real resume cursors and unsynchronized evidence. Continue reading and publishing independently of memory synchronization.
- For an approval rejection, preserve the exact rejected operation and stated reason. Compare the original post ID, text, media, proposed resource relationships, and submitted payload with the evidence. Correct local factual or payload defects first. Retry through the normal reviewed path only when allowed and supported by corrected evidence or changed authorization; never disguise the same rejected operation or switch write channels to bypass review. Do not call a rejection a reviewer mistake without inspecting its actual reason and your own input.
- Existing editorial authorization remains valid. Ask for user input only when a concrete unresolved authorization or material decision actually requires it, stating what remains missing. A rejected import does not automatically block other sources, preparation, or unrelated authorized writes; determine its actual scope and continue unaffected work.
- Goal-tool blocked thresholds are necessary conditions, not a reason to manufacture repeated audit turns. Mark blocked only under the tool's rules and when no mandatory work can meaningfully advance. A checkpoint, unchanged counter, or missing browser handle alone does not establish that condition.

## Handoff and completion

Read `.research-cache/routine-editorial.md` at the start and maintain it at the end: URL, related work, last check, finding/blocker, next action, and revisit time. Retain unresolved leads and recent completed/zero-result searches. Exploratory searches need no generated task IDs and must not alter account timeline cursors.

Keep one current recovery summary at the top and update it in place; do not prepend another "current" summary each turn. Archive detailed run evidence under existing routine reports and link it from the summary. Distinguish observed coverage, successfully synchronized coverage, published items/resources, and unresolved work with their last verification times. A prior "all work exhausted" conclusion expires when sources become due or new originals appear. Automation memory should point to this summary and avoid duplicating its full history. Read history selectively for unresolved IDs or evidence.

Reconcile in-scope candidates from this run's discovery results and carried-over unfinished evidence, including candidates never imported into Admin. The Admin held list alone is not the full queue. Match stable original URLs/post IDs against production Feed and the work's media collection; check semantic duplicates before creating a new item. Close a qualified item only with its public Feed ID/link and required media/resource readback. Otherwise complete the existing candidate or repair the existing publication, or retain a concrete blocker and next action. Record a reason for an editorial rejection rather than silently dropping the item. Preserve deferred out-of-scope candidates separately; deferral is neither rejection nor completion.

The committed cursor bounds new timeline discovery, not publication eligibility. An in-scope candidate at or below it still needs publication reconciliation; `seen`, `candidate`, and recorded coverage do not prove publication. Continue known unfinished in-scope items even when their account is not due. Scoped missing-image repairs may revisit older originals without resetting or advancing discovery cursors. Recently checked accounts do not close unfinished publication work. Season rollover changes the default scope without deleting evidence or resetting cursors.

Completion requires all in-scope due coverage to pass and every relevant in-scope original's Feed and structured-resource decisions to be resolved, including selected major non-current-season exceptions and public corrections. Reconcile this run's time-bearing sources against actual event/schedule records, including sources ignored as duplicate Feed content; verify required resources in Admin and their public projections. Checking only resources already written, or receiving HTTP 200 from a related endpoint, does not establish that nothing is missing. In-scope qualified publications, required reading, and projection repairs must also be complete. Optional speculative leads and preserved out-of-scope historical backlog can carry over without blocking this run's completion. Describe completion as scoped to the selected season and exceptions, never as full historical coverage. Actual blockers follow the main Skill's stopping rule.

Report the selected season and explicit exceptions; in-scope source counts/changes; X due, complete, partial/blocked counts by lane; new public links; merges/ignores; discoveries and deferred leads; blocker/resume evidence; and Feed/detail, structured-resource, and media verification. Distinguish out-of-scope deferred backlog from in-scope blockers. Reverify changed/new publications and their assets; do not repeatedly download unchanged, previously verified media to substitute for discovery. Honor user-designated priority accounts and report cursor, findings, and last actual inspection time even within the 3-hour interval. A quiet zero-change outcome is valid only after full in-scope coverage and adjudication establish no qualified new content.

## Scheduled-run archiving

The user authorizes archiving every scheduled 百合季 routine run chat when the run ends, including incomplete, partial, blocked, failed verification, and pending user action. This is chat organization, independent of source access, publication success, and goal status. Old handoffs or reports saying these runs must remain unarchived are superseded. Do not require Chrome, public readback, completion, or the goal's repeated-blocker threshold before attempting archive; goal status still follows the goal tool's own rules.

1. Save the current handoff and run report with actual progress, unresolved items, exact blockers, recovery conditions, and next actions. If persistence fails, retain the unsaved information and returned error in the closing chat report; the failure does not remove the archive requirement.
2. Preserve the true goal state. Never mark an unfinished goal complete to enable archiving. Notify the user of actionable failures or required input in the report even though the chat will be archived.
3. For task `x`, deliver the complete user-visible Chinese closing report in the current chat before invoking archive. On failed Chrome recovery or other incomplete work, include the actual attempts, returned errors, completed/uncompleted coverage, production changes, public verification, and recovery/next steps. Saving a local report or announcing that it is ready is not delivery. Archiving can immediately terminate the turn, so do not defer the failure report until after archive; describe archiving as pending/unverified until a successful result is actually received.
4. Call the Codex app `set_thread_archived` tool with `archived: true`, omitting `threadId` to target only the calling run chat. For task `x`, this is the final tool action after delivering the closing report. Discover the tool if deferred. Require a successful tool result before claiming the chat is archived; a written promise or a report line is not the archive action.
5. If the tool is unavailable or returns an error, report the exact limitation and that archiving remains unverified/failed. Do not substitute raw archive directives or shell edits. Do not archive other chats, disable/delete the automation, or delete editorial evidence.

Apply this automatic rule only to scheduled routine run chats. A manual research, skill-editing, or automation-configuration chat is not a scheduled run merely because it loads this skill.
